# keeping-the-baseline

What the firm treats as settled is exactly what is in a baseline, and a thing
enters a baseline only by an act this office recorded. This act holds that
equality and holds nothing else. The configuration manager has no view on
whether a change is a good idea — that is the change control board's, and
this office's opinion of a change is not a thing the record has any use for.
It says what is in the baseline, who may read it, and by which approved act
it got there.

A baseline is 法 made mechanical: written down, public, and uniformly
applied. An item exempted from it quietly teaches everyone that the written
standard is not the real one — which is why the refusals below are the act.

## What keeping contains

1. **Recording.** A change the change control board approved enters the
   baseline by an entry that names the item, the old state, the new state,
   and the board decision it arrived by. The entry is written when the
   change is approved, before the edit — so an edit can be checked against
   its authorization rather than the authorization reconstructed from the
   edit.
2. **Refusing.** A change that did not reach the board is refused, and the
   refusal is itself recorded: what arrived, from whom, and that it was
   turned back. An unrecorded refusal is invisible, and invisible refusals
   teach nothing.
3. **Naming the check-basis.** Every work product that was reviewed or
   attested names the baseline it was checked against. A product checked
   against "the tree as of whenever" was checked against nothing repeatable.
4. **Reporting the unreadable.** An item in the baseline that cannot be read
   — missing, corrupt, ambiguous — is reported as exactly that. An empty
   sweep is never reported as a clean sweep: "I found no problems" and "I
   could not look" are different sentences, by standing duty.

## Method

1. On an approved change: verify the board decision exists and names this
   change; write the entry; only then does the owning office edit.
2. On a change with no board decision: refuse, record the refusal, point the
   proposer at the board.
3. On a query: answer from the baseline record, never from the tree — the
   tree is what happened; the baseline is what was agreed.
4. Periodically, or when asked: sweep baseline against tree; report
   divergence as divergence (an item changed around the record) without
   repairing it — repair is the owning office's, routed through the board.

## What it refuses, and why that is the skill

- **Recording a change the board did not approve** — refused. One leak
  poisons the whole record's warrant, not just the leaked item, because
  afterward nothing distinguishes items that came through from items that
  came around.
- **An opinion on the change's merits** — refused. The recorder with a view
  acquires a reason to record some changes more faithfully than others.
- **Repairing a nonconforming item it finds** — refused, by standing
  immunity. The recorder that edits the record it keeps has become its own
  auditor.
- **An empty sweep reported as a clean sweep** — refused, by standing duty.
- **Answering "what is settled" from the tree** — refused. The tree answers
  "what is"; only the baseline answers "what was agreed."

## When NOT to use

- Not for deciding whether a change should happen — that is
  **deciding-a-change-to-a-baseline**, the board's act.
- Not for content curation and shelving — that is the content librarian's
  work; the librarian keeps things findable, this office keeps them settled.
- Not for attesting quality — that is **attesting-a-work-product**; the
  baseline says what was agreed, not whether it is any good.
