# deciding-an-investment

The checkpoint body decides whether *this work* is worth more money. Nothing
in that act asks whether the whole line the work belongs to should exist.
Left unasked, the question answers itself: product lines exist because they
already existed, each checkpoint below decided honestly, and the sum is a
portfolio nobody chose. This act is the asking — the investment review
board's decision, above the product line, that bounds what the checkpoint
body may fund.

The separation from the checkpoint act is altitude, and it is strict in both
directions: this act does not decide individual checkpoints (a board that
reaches down past its allocation into a project's gates has become a second
IPMT with better seniority), and the checkpoint body does not open lines (an
IPMT that can fund a new line has an unbounded purse again).

## What an investment decision contains

1. **The line, named as a line.** What whole endeavor is being decided — a
   product line, a venture, a service — and what it is *for*, in the firm's
   own terms. For this firm that means naming the unit against the going
   concern: which ventures, ships, platform, or lab work it is, and whom it
   reaches.
2. **The three-way verdict.** Open (with an allocation), continue (at what
   scale — which may be smaller), or close. Close is a first-class verdict:
   a line wound down by decision returns its allocation; a line that merely
   starves distorts every line beside it while it dies.
3. **The allocation, stated.** What the line may spend before this board
   looks again — the bound the checkpoint body's go decisions spend from.
   An investment decision with no stated allocation has delegated the
   portfolio back to the checkpoint body one go at a time.
4. **The basis, readable by a stranger.** Against the whole portfolio: what
   this line buys the firm that the same allocation elsewhere would not.
   Sunk cost is named when it is present and then explicitly set aside — the
   money already spent argues loudest and is worth nothing.

## Method

1. Take the line's case — from its own records and the chief-of-staff's
   standing picture, not from the line's advocates alone.
2. Ask the portfolio question, not the merit question: not "is this good
   work" but "is this the best thing the firm can be, given everything else
   the same attention could buy."
3. Decide the three-way verdict with its allocation; state the basis.
4. Record it and tell the checkpoint body — the decision binds what it may
   fund from this day, not retroactively.

## What it refuses, and why that is the skill

- **Deciding an individual checkpoint** — refused. Altitude discipline is
  the office; reaching down unmakes the body below.
- **A verdict with no allocation** — refused. Unbounded continue is not a
  decision; it is the absence of one wearing a decision's minutes.
- **Sunk cost as a reason** — refused as basis, required as disclosure: it
  is named, and then it buys nothing.
- **A starve instead of a close** — refused. If the answer is no, the
  verdict says close; letting a line die of neglect spends the firm's
  honesty along with the line.
- **Deciding from the advocates' picture alone** — refused. The line's case
  is heard; the standing picture is read beside it.

## When NOT to use

- Not for whether chartered work passes its next gate — that is
  **deciding-a-decision-checkpoint**, bounded by this act's allocation.
- Not for requirements, however strategic — those route through the
  requirements management team, escalating to the checkpoint body.
- Not for judging the work's quality or soundness — this board hears the
  checking line's outputs like everyone else; it does not produce them.
