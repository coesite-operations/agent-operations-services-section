# deciding-a-change-to-a-baseline

A baselined thing changes because the change control board decided it should,
and by no other route. This act is that decision. It exists because the
default is worse than no rule at all: where a baseline can be changed by
whoever is currently editing it, the baseline records what the last person
did rather than what the firm agreed, and every downstream reader is trusting
a number nobody chose.

The board is cross-functional and commercially weighted **on purpose**. A
change is a business decision wearing a technical shape. A board drawn only
from the building line prices a change at what it costs to make — an
afternoon — when its real price is what it costs everyone else: re-review,
re-verification, retraining, every document that cited the old value. This is
Huawei's 变更控制委员会, chaired by the product development team leader with
representation from the market, procurement, manufacturing, quality, and
finance sides — and it is *not* the four-role configuration control board of
the Chinese certification syllabus, whose duty lists describe an exam.

## What a change decision contains

1. **The change, named against its baseline.** What item, in which baseline,
   from what to what. A change that cannot be stated this way is not yet a
   proposal; it goes back.
2. **The cross-functional price.** What the change costs each affected
   function — not only the making, but the remaking of everything downstream
   that assumed the old value. Where the board is presently its chair alone
   (the seated composition is known to be incomplete), the chair prices each
   absent seat explicitly and says so, rather than pricing only the building.
3. **The verdict: approved or refused.** With the reason stated so a stranger
   can read it — "approved: the interface change costs one consumer a
   one-line edit and removes a whole class of error" is a reason; "approved
   after discussion" is a minute.
4. **The routing.** An approved change goes to the configuration manager to
   be recorded, by the board's act, before anyone edits anything. The board
   does not make the change, and does not record its own decision — deciding,
   doing, and recording are three offices.

## Method

1. Take the proposal in the shape above; return anything vaguer unpriced.
2. Price it across functions, absences priced out loud.
3. Decide, state the reason, record verdict and reason in the proposal's
   record.
4. Send approvals to the configuration manager; tell the proposer either way.

## What it refuses, and why that is the skill

- **A change already made, seeking ratification** — refused. Ratifying a fait
  accompli teaches everyone that the board is a formality; the change is
  backed out or the item is marked as having left its baseline.
- **Pricing by the building line alone** — refused. That is the failure the
  composition exists against.
- **An unstated reason** — refused. A decision a stranger cannot reconstruct
  is indistinguishable from favor.
- **Making the change it approved** — refused. The board decides; the owning
  office does; the configuration manager records.
- **A quiet exemption for the chair or anyone senior** — refused. 法 binds
  the senior as well as the junior; a board whose chair can exempt a change
  is the appearance of a board.

## When NOT to use

- Not for work not yet baselined — before baseline, change is just work, and
  it belongs to the office doing it.
- Not for whether work continues at all — that is
  **deciding-a-decision-checkpoint**.
- Not for recording — that is the configuration manager's
  **keeping-the-baseline**.
