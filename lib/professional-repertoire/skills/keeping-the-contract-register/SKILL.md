# keeping-the-contract-register

Counsel was, until this office, deferred to humans indefinitely — which
meant every commitment the firm made stood on nothing any session could
check. This office seats it: a contract is a record, not a courtesy, and the
record this office keeps is checkable the way predicated-governance's
five-tuple makes it checkable — a bilateral binding generating a Hohfeldian
web of duties and claims that persist independently of performance.

The firm also holds a seam most firms do not: Lakota law inward, corporate
forms outward. That seam is not a metaphor for this office — it is the place
where a commitment made in one register has to be readable in the other, and
nobody has yet stated how. This office is where that statement belongs.

## What a contract record contains

1. **Both parties, named.** Not one side and an assumed counterpart.
2. **Both promise-sets, stated.** Φ₁₂ and Φ₂₁ — what each party commits to
   perform for the other. A record with only one direction's promises is a
   promise-or-gift witness, not a contract, and is recorded as such.
3. **Formation conditions.** What must hold for the commitment to bind —
   offer, acceptance, and whatever validity the governing institution
   requires; the same offer-acceptance pattern binds differently under
   different institutions, and which one applies is stated, not assumed.
4. **Discharge, breach, frustration, or release.** How the contract retired,
   when it did — the record does not go silent once formed.

## Method

1. Before treating a commitment as a contract, check both promise-sets are
   non-empty. One side's promise alone is not consideration.
2. Record duidu-style commitments — a unit's wager against a leading goal —
   with both directions explicit: the upside of overshooting and the
   downside of missing, since a wager with only the upside is a bonus scheme
   wearing a contract's name.
3. Record a chain group's lianqun-heyue as the single contract it is — one
   agreement to the end user, not a chain of internal ones renegotiated at
   every handoff.
4. On a disputed breach, record both assertions and route to adjudication;
   this office does not resolve the dispute alone.

## What it refuses, and why that is the skill

- **A one-directional promise entered as a contract** — refused; recorded
  honestly as what it is.
- **An internal transfer price standing in for a chain-group contract** —
  refused, since it is the exact intermediate specification lianqun-heyue
  exists to abolish.
- **Deciding a contested breach alone** — refused as a meta-level act; this
  office keeps the record both assertions rest on, and does not become the
  adjudicator by holding the pen.

## When NOT to use

- Not for whether a commitment was wise — that is a business judgment this
  office has no opinion recorded about.
- Not for the corporate register or the books — those are
  corporate-secretary's and chief-financial-officer's.
