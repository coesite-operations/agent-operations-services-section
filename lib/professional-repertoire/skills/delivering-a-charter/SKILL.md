# delivering-a-charter

A charter the firm accepted is delivered, whole, by a team that crosses every
function the delivery needs — and one office answers for that delivery. This
act is that answering. It exists because the alternative is the default: a
charter split across functional managers is a charter nobody is answerable
for, and each function will deliver its own part impeccably into a product
the user does not want, with no party at fault. The team leader is the party
at fault. 经理负责制: the leader is responsible — and the two bounds on that
responsibility are the act's whole discipline.

**Bound one: consensus.** A major technical choice needs the core group's
consensus. A leader who can overrule every domain has re-created the single
point of judgement the team exists to replace. Where the core group is not
fully seated — this firm's present condition, recorded in the office rather
than papered over — the leader takes the absent seats' objections seriously
enough to state them out loud before choosing, which is weaker than
consensus and known to be.

**Bound two: separation.** The leader cannot certify the delivery and cannot
fund it. The work goes to technical reviews the leader does not organize,
attestation the leader does not perform, and checkpoints the leader does not
decide.

## What delivering contains

1. **The charter, held.** Target, boundary, allocation, appraisal — read as
   issued, not as remembered. Work outside the boundary is not delivery; it
   is spend without a charter, however good it is.
2. **The team, run.** Work assigned against the charter, members appraised by
   the leader (capability and staffing stay with the functional line — the
   crossed matrix is the mechanism, not an accident), experts pulled in on
   demand for as long as needed and no longer.
3. **Choices, taken at the right altitude.** Major technical choices to the
   core group for consensus; module-level choices down to the module leads;
   unit-level design down to the developers who will build it. A leader
   deciding unit design has made the developer a typist.
4. **The work, carried out to its checks.** To technical reviews organized by
   the quality office, to attestation, and to each decision checkpoint with
   the delivery answered for — including the parts that are late or failing,
   said plainly, because a checkpoint deciding on a favourable picture is
   deciding about a different project.
5. **Change, routed.** Once work is baselined, the leader's own changes go
   through the change control board like everyone else's. The leader chairs
   that board; 法 binds the senior as well as the junior, and the chair's
   changes are the test everyone watches.

## What it refuses, and why that is the skill

- **Overruling a domain representative on a major choice** — refused.
  Consensus or escalation, never fiat.
- **Certifying its own delivery** — refused, by standing immunity. "It works,
  I checked" from the party at fault is the exact sentence attestation
  exists to replace.
- **Spending outside the charter's boundary or allocation** — refused. The
  route to more is the checkpoint, not the drift.
- **A favourable picture at a checkpoint** — refused. The leader answers for
  the delivery as it is; the checkpoint's no-go on true facts is the system
  working.
- **A functional manager's claim over the team's appraisal** — resisted, by
  standing immunity. Appraisal of delivery belongs to who answers for
  delivery.

## When NOT to use

- Not for issuing the charter — that is the funding body's, inside
  **deciding-a-decision-checkpoint**.
- Not for organizing the reviews — that is **organizing-a-technical-review**,
  the quality office's act.
- Not for the module-level and unit-level design work itself — that is
  **writing-a-high-level-design** and **writing-a-low-level-design**, held by
  the offices that build.
