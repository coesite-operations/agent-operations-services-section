Rendanheyi (人单合一) names a management model developed at the Haier Group under Zhang Ruimin beginning in 2005, and the organizational form that results from applying it.

The name is a compressed statement of the model's content. 人 (rén) is the person — the individual employee. 单 (dān) is the user's value: not a sales order in the accounting sense, but the need of a specific identified user that the person's work exists to meet. 合一 (hé yī) is to be made one. The claim is that these two things should not be separate objects joined by a chain of management, but a single object: the person *is* the user value they create, and can be described no other way.

The mechanism that gives this content is compensation. In the model, an employee's pay is a function of the user value they created, not of their position, their tenure, or their manager's assessment. Haier states this as 薪酬来自用户 — salary comes from users. Its practical form is that value-added pay falls to zero when no user value is created, regardless of effort expended or hours worked. Everything else in the model is machinery for making that statement true rather than rhetorical: the enterprise is broken into small units small enough to be attributed a user, those units are given the rights needed to act without permission, and their accounts are kept in a form that measures user value rather than internal throughput.

Historically the model was introduced incrementally over roughly two decades and restructured several times. The best-known step is the 2013–2014 reorganization, which eliminated some ten thousand middle-management positions and reconstituted the company as several thousand micro-enterprises. Subsequent restructurings — most significantly the 2019 introduction of chain groups — changed the grouping of those units substantially, and the model's vocabulary is not stable across these periods.

The concept's boundaries are worth stating, because the model is frequently described loosely. Rendanheyi is not decentralization, flat structure, or the removal of hierarchy as such; it replaces the hierarchy with a market, which is a different and more demanding arrangement than an absence of authority. It is not employee empowerment or engagement, which are attitudes the company cultivates in employees, whereas Rendanheyi is a change in who pays them. It is not internal entrepreneurship in the sense of permitting side projects, since the model's units are the ordinary operating units of the firm and not exceptions to it. And it is not agile or lean practice: those govern how work is done within a unit, while Rendanheyi governs what a unit is and where its income comes from.

The model is claimed by Haier as generalizable and has been deliberately propagated to acquired firms, notably GE Appliances after its 2016 acquisition, and taught externally through the Rendanheyi research network. Whether it transfers is contested; the model's own answer is that it transfers only where the acquiring firm is willing to give up the authority to overrule the market it has created.
