A xiaowei (小微), conventionally rendered in English as a micro-enterprise or ME, is the basic operating unit of the Rendanheyi model: a small team, typically on the order of ten to twenty people, constituted as an enterprise rather than as a department.

The distinction between an enterprise and a department is the whole of the definition, and it is not a matter of degree. A department has a budget, a manager it reports to, and an internal customer it serves; it is funded whether or not anyone values its output, and it is corrected by instruction from above. A micro-enterprise has its own profit-and-loss accounts, its own hiring and firing, its own authority to set and distribute pay, a contract committing it to targets, and income that arrives only when a user is served. It is corrected by its results. A unit that has been renamed but still draws a budget and answers to a manager is a department, and the model treats the distinction as binary rather than aspirational.

Three kinds are named in Haier's taxonomy, distinguished by whose need constitutes their 单. Market micro-enterprises (市场小微) face an end user directly and own a product or a user base. Node micro-enterprises (节点小微) serve other micro-enterprises — design, manufacturing, logistics, personnel, information technology — and reach the end user only through the units they supply. Incubating micro-enterprises (孵化小微) are new ventures pursuing a user that does not yet exist for the firm.

The unit's constitution consists of a small number of specific instruments, each of which is a term in its own right. It holds three rights (三权) — decision, personnel, and distribution — delegated in full rather than subject to review. It signs a value-adjustment contract (对赌) committing it to targets benchmarked outside the firm. It keeps a win-win value-added statement (共赢增值表) in place of a conventional profit-and-loss account. Its leadership is contestable: any party may bid for it by grabbing the order (抢单), and the team may remove the holder by mutual selection (官兵互选). Its members are paid from user-created value (人单酬).

The count of these units is often quoted — several thousand at Haier following the 2013–2014 restructuring — and is the least informative fact about the model. The number reflects a decomposition; the model's content is in what each decomposed piece is permitted and required to do.

The concept's boundaries: a micro-enterprise is not a team, a squad, a pod, or a cell, all of which are units of work organization inside a firm that retains the payroll. It is not a subsidiary, which is a legal entity under a parent's control with a board appointed by that parent. It is not a franchise, since it is not licensed a proven formula and is not obligated to run it. It is a firm-shaped thing operating inside another firm, and it is only that if the market it faces is real enough to kill it.
