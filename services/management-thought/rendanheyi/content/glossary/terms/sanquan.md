Sanquan (三权), the three rights, names the authority delegated in full to a micro-enterprise under Rendanheyi: 决策权 the right of decision, 用人权 the right of personnel, and 分配权 the right of distribution.

The right of decision is the authority to determine what the unit does — what it builds, what it charges, whom it serves, which opportunities it takes and declines — without submitting the determination for approval. The right of personnel is the authority to select the unit's members, to add them and remove them, including the selection of its own leader. The right of distribution is the authority to divide the unit's earnings among its members by whatever formula it sets.

The three are stated as a set because the set is what constitutes an enterprise, and any two of them do not. A unit that decides and hires but cannot set pay has had its incentives written by someone else and will act on those incentives rather than on its judgment. A unit that decides and pays but cannot choose its members has been given an objective and denied the means. A unit that hires and pays but cannot decide is a contractor. Partial delegation is the ordinary condition of a well-run department, and the model's claim is that it produces a department every time, regardless of what the unit is called or how much latitude it is granted, because latitude granted is latitude that can be withdrawn by the party who granted it.

The third right is the one firms find hardest to concede and the one that carries most of the model's weight. Distribution authority means the unit's earnings are the unit's, to divide as its members agree, and that a member may out-earn people far senior to them elsewhere in the firm if the unit's results warrant it. Compensation bands, internal equity policies, and approval thresholds on pay are all withdrawals of this right, and each of them reattaches the member's income to their position in the firm — which is the precise linkage the model exists to sever.

The rights are paired with exposure rather than granted freely. A unit holding them signs a value-adjustment contract, keeps accounts that show what it created, and can be dissolved by its results. The model's formulation is that the rights are not a gift from the hierarchy but the ordinary equipment of anything expected to survive on its own earnings; a firm that wants the accountability without conceding the rights has asked a unit to be responsible for outcomes it cannot cause.

The concept's boundaries: the three rights are not empowerment or autonomy, which describe how much room a manager allows a team and are revocable at that manager's discretion. They are not delegation in the ordinary managerial sense, since delegated authority remains the delegator's and is exercised on their behalf. They are the rights an enterprise holds because it is an enterprise, and the model's test is not how broad they are but whether there exists any party who can overrule their exercise.
