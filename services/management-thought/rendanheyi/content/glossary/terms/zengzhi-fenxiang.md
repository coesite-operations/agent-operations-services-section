Zengzhi fenxiang (增值分享), value-added sharing, is the division of surplus created above a committed target among the parties to a chain-group contract. It is the mechanism by which a leading goal that has been overshot becomes income for the people who overshot it.

Its object is the increment, not the total. Revenue up to the committed target funds the unit's operation and its base pay; only value created beyond that target enters the shared pool. This makes the target a threshold rather than a quota — a unit that merely reaches its goal has earned nothing to share, which is the intended reading and the reason the goal is set where reaching it is not assured.

Its distribution is the model's structurally distinctive feature. The pool is divided among every party to the contract, which includes micro-enterprises that never touched the end user, and — where the arrangement extends beyond the firm — outside participants: suppliers, developers, and ecosystem partners who contributed to the outcome. Sharing beyond the firm's own payroll follows from the 共赢 commitment: an arrangement in which outside parties supply value and the firm keeps the entire surplus is one those parties correctly decline to keep entering, and the model treats the firm's ability to attract them as an asset it must pay to maintain.

The sharing is symmetric with shortfall. A group that misses its target has no pool, and this applies to every constituent unit regardless of individual contribution — a node that performed its own part flawlessly inside a group that failed the user shares in the failure. The two directions are one rule, and the downside is what makes the upside a claim on a common outcome rather than a bonus.

Within a unit, the unit's own share is divided by its members under the distribution right, on whatever formula they set. There is no company-wide schedule governing this, which is deliberate: a central formula would make the division a company decision and thereby restore the company as the payer.

The concept's boundaries: value-added sharing is not profit-sharing, which divides an aggregate the company determines after the fact and typically restricts to employees. It is not gainsharing, which distributes cost savings within a plant or unit. It is not a bonus pool, which is funded at the firm's discretion and can be withheld without any structural consequence. The distinguishing features are that the pool is defined by a contract rather than by a decision, that it can be empty, and that it does not stop at the firm's boundary.
