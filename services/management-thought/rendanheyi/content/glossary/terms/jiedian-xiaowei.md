A jiedian xiaowei (节点小微), or node micro-enterprise, is one of the three kinds of micro-enterprise in the Rendanheyi model: the kind whose immediate customer is another micro-enterprise rather than an end user. Design, manufacturing, logistics, personnel, and information technology units take this form.

The form exists because most work in a firm of any size does not touch a customer. A model whose central commitment is that pay comes from users must therefore say something about the majority of its own units, and saying "those are overhead" would concede the point. The node micro-enterprise is that answer.

Its central difficulty is immediate. Rendanheyi's founding requirement is zero distance (零距离) between the person working and the user whose value they create. A unit whose user is a colleague satisfies this only nominally: its customer cannot leave, cannot be lost, and is not spending their own money, so serving that customer well and serving them profitably come apart. A unit in that position optimizes for the internal customer's approval, which is a political rather than an economic signal. This is precisely the condition the model names large-enterprise disease (大企业病), and a node micro-enterprise that stops at its internal customer has reproduced the cost center it was meant to replace.

The model's resolution is that the node's 单 passes through the internal customer to the end user rather than terminating at them. The node is wired in parallel (并联) into a chain group, and the chain group holds a single contract to the end user in which every constituent unit is a party. The node's income is therefore a share of the value-added the whole chain creates for that end user, not a transfer from the internal customer's budget. Stated as a rule: the internal micro-enterprise is who the node *serves*; the end user is who *pays* it. Where this holds, the node is an enterprise. Where the node is paid by its internal customer out of an allocation, it is a supplier at best and a department at worst, whatever it is called.

The second and load-bearing instrument is bilateral market exposure, and it runs in both directions. The node must be permitted to sell its output outside the ecosystem entirely, to firms with no relationship to the parent. Its internal customers must be permitted to buy the same capability outside, from anyone. Only the conjunction does the work. Selling outside without internal customers being free to leave produces a unit that is subsidized at home and competitive abroad; internal customers being free to leave without the node being free to sell outside produces a unit that can only shrink. Together they mean the node's internal customers are customers rather than captives, which is the only condition under which serving them is evidence of anything.

The node micro-enterprise is the position from which a platform may emerge. Where a node's output becomes general enough that many units and eventually outside firms build on it, the node's character changes and its owner comes to be graded on the total value-added created atop it rather than on its own margin. Haier's central case is COSMOPlat, which began as internal manufacturing capability, served other Haier units as a node, and was subsequently exposed as an industrial platform sold across unrelated industries. The transition is not automatic and not a matter of naming: a node becomes a platform when there is enough built on it that grading its owner on ecosystem-wide value-added is a meaningful instrument rather than an aspiration.
