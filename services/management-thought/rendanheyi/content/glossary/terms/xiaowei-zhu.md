A xiaowei zhu (小微主) is the party who holds a micro-enterprise: who exercises its three rights, signs its value-adjustment contract, and bears the result in both directions. The English rendering is micro-enterprise owner or ME owner; leader is also used and is weaker than the Chinese, since 主 carries the sense of proprietor rather than of appointed head.

The position is taken and not given. It is acquired by 抢单 — bidding for it against other bidders by committing to a target — and it is held only while the unit's members sustain the holder under 官兵互选 and the results sustain the contract. There is consequently no party who can appoint a xiaowei zhu, and this is a structural fact rather than a procedural preference: a position that could be conferred could be withdrawn by the conferring party, which would make that party the holder's real counterparty and reinstate everything the model removed. A firm that wishes to install someone as a micro-enterprise owner has misunderstood the term.

The position's authority is genuine and is not consensus-bound. Having been selected, the holder decides — what the unit does, who is in it, how the money divides — without the members' assent to any particular exercise and without approval from above under 零签字. The members' power is over tenure, not over decisions. This combination, full authority within the role and full contestability of the role, is the position's characteristic shape, and each half is unsafe without the other: authority without contestability is ordinary management, contestability without authority is a leader who cannot act.

The exposure is symmetric with the authority. The holder's income is the unit's result, not a salary attached to the position. A missed target reduces or eliminates it; a sustained miss ends the tenure or dissolves the unit. Owners are ordinarily required to invest their own money in the unit, most stringently in incubating micro-enterprises, so that the downside is not merely a foregone upside.

The position is an office rather than a person, in the ordinary sense that it is defined by its rights, its contract, and its exposure, and persists across whoever holds it. Its occupant changes by the mechanisms above, and the unit's obligations do not lapse when they do.

The concept's boundaries: a xiaowei zhu is not a general manager or business-unit head, who is appointed, salaried, and removable by the appointer. It is not a founder, since the position is contestable and the founder's tenure in an ordinary company is not. It is not an intrapreneur, which names a disposition permitted within a firm rather than a set of rights held against it. And it is not an elected representative, since the holder governs rather than represents — the electorate chooses who holds the rights, not how they are used.
