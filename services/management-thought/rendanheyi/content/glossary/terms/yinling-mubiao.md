A yinling mubiao (引领目标), a leading goal, is a target set by reference to the best comparable performance available anywhere in the market rather than by increment from the unit's own prior results. It is the quantity a micro-enterprise commits to in its value-adjustment contract.

The distinction is against the incremental target, which is the ordinary instrument of corporate planning: last year's number, adjusted. The model's objection to it is not that it is unambitious but that it measures the wrong thing. An incremental target compares a unit to its own past, which has two specific consequences. A business in structural decline meets its incremental targets continuously while dying, because each year's target is derived from the last year's fall. And a unit that performed badly is rewarded for it, since its poor prior year becomes the low base from which its next target is set — making a bad year an asset.

A leading goal removes the unit's own history from the calculation. The reference is external and absolute: what the best operator anywhere achieves, whether or not that operator is in the same industry, and whether or not the unit has any current prospect of matching it. The target may therefore be one the unit has no idea how to reach at the moment it signs.

This is intentional and is the concept's substantive claim. A target derived from what a unit knows how to do can be met by doing what it already does, and therefore never requires the unit to change what it is. A target that exceeds current capability cannot be met by execution and must be met by finding a different way to operate — which is the outcome the instrument exists to force. The model accepts as a cost that a substantial fraction of such targets will be missed, on the view that a target set where it is comfortably reachable has purchased its own reliability by giving up its function.

The obvious hazard is that a target nobody can reach stops motivating anyone, and a unit that concludes its goal is unreachable will optimize for the shortfall or for its own exit. The model's counterweight is that the goal is entered voluntarily under 抢单 — nobody is assigned one — and that its holder can decline. Whether this is a sufficient answer is a fair question the public accounts do not settle.

The concept's boundaries: a leading goal is not a stretch goal, which is an incremental target with a margin added and is still derived from the unit's history. It is not a benchmark, which is a measurement of where others stand rather than a commitment. And it is not a vision or an aspiration, since it is the specific number in a contract with a consequence attached.
