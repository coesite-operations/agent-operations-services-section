A fuhua xiaowei (孵化小微), or incubating micro-enterprise, is the kind of micro-enterprise pursuing a user the firm does not yet have. Where a market micro-enterprise transforms an existing business and a node micro-enterprise supplies other units, an incubating micro-enterprise is a new venture attempting to establish that a user and a business exist at all.

Its distinguishing instrument is how it is funded. It does not receive an internal budget, which would make it a corporate project and place its fate in the judgment of whoever approved it. It raises staged investment, and the model requires that this investment be at least partly external — venture capital from outside the firm, on that investor's own terms and at that investor's own risk. Its own team is normally required to invest personal money alongside. The parent firm's capital is one participant among these rather than the source.

The reason for the external requirement is evidentiary rather than financial; the firm is not short of money. A venture funded internally is validated by an internal opinion, and internal opinion about a new business is the least reliable instrument any large firm possesses. An outside investor who has no relationship to the firm, no stake in its politics, and money at risk is a test of the proposition rather than of the proposer. The team's own money serves the same function from the other direction: it makes the downside of a venture nobody outside wants fall on the people proposing it.

Consequently the incubating micro-enterprise is the only kind whose ownership is expected to leave. Successful incubations dilute the parent, raise further rounds, and may list independently; Thunderobot, begun by a small internal group and grown into a separately listed company, is the standard example. The model treats this as the intended outcome rather than as a loss of control, on the reasoning that a firm which cannot let a venture go cannot credibly claim to have funded it rather than assigned it.

Its constitution is otherwise the standard one — three rights, a value-adjustment contract, a win-win value-added statement, user-derived pay — and its targets are the most aggressively external of the three kinds, since it has no internal history to be benchmarked against.

The concept's boundaries: an incubating micro-enterprise is not an innovation lab, a skunkworks, or an internal accelerator, all of which are internally funded and therefore internally judged. It is not a corporate venture capital arm, which invests in other people's companies. It is a company being started, by the people starting it, with the parent as an investor rather than an owner.
