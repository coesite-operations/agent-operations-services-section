Guanbing huxuan (官兵互选) — mutual selection of officers and soldiers — is the rule that selection within a micro-enterprise runs in both directions. The leader chooses the members, which is unremarkable. The members choose the leader, and can subsequently remove them, which is the term's content.

The compound's imagery is military and the inversion is deliberate: 官, officers, and 兵, soldiers, name the relationship in which one-directional selection is most taken for granted, and the term asserts that in a micro-enterprise it runs both ways.

The rule operates alongside 抢单. Bidding is how a candidate offers to take a unit's leadership; mutual selection is how the unit's members decide among bidders and how they subsequently keep or discharge the one they chose. Removal is not a grievance process, an appeal to a higher authority, or a request that someone else act. The members remove the leader; there is no party above the unit whose ratification is required, and none to whom a removed leader appeals.

The mechanism's function is to make the leader's position depend on the people doing the work rather than on the people above them. Under one-directional selection a leader's tenure is a function of their superior's assessment, which makes the superior the rational object of the leader's attention, and produces the ordinary pathology in which a unit is managed upward while its members' knowledge of what is going wrong has nowhere to go. Selection running both ways makes the members' judgment consequential without requiring them to escalate it — which is also why the model can dispense with a middle-management layer whose classical function is to carry that judgment upward.

The rule reaches membership as well as leadership. Members are not assigned to a unit and cannot be transferred into one over its objection; equally, a member the unit does not want does not remain, and a member who does not want the unit leaves for another. A person nobody's unit will take is not carried, which is the rule's harshest consequence and is not softened in the model's accounts.

The concept's boundaries: mutual selection is not a 360-degree review, which collects subordinates' opinions as input to a decision that a superior still makes. It is not upward feedback or an engagement survey, both of which are information-gathering without a consequence attached. It is not a works council or union representation, which negotiate with an authority that remains in place. And it is not consensus or democratic management generally, since the leader once selected holds the three rights and decides — the members' authority is over who holds the position, not over its exercise.
