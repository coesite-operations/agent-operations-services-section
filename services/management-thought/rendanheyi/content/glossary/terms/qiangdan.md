Qiangdan (抢单), literally to grab or seize the order, is the mechanism by which leadership of a micro-enterprise is taken rather than assigned. When a unit needs a leader — because one is being formed, or because the incumbent has been removed or has failed a target — the position is opened and any party may bid for it.

A bid is not an application, and this is the term's substance. A candidate does not present qualifications for someone's assessment; they state the target they will commit to and the plan by which they will reach it, and in bidding they accept the value-adjustment contract that goes with it. The bid is therefore a wager the candidate is offering to enter, and the competition is among wagers rather than among résumés. Candidates may come from inside the unit, from elsewhere in the firm, or from outside it entirely; the model does not privilege incumbency, seniority, or internal candidacy.

The mechanism's function is to move selection from judgment to commitment. Ordinary appointment asks a superior to predict who will perform, which is a forecast made from evidence about the past, and it produces the well-documented pathologies of that forecast: promotion by tenure, by visibility to the appointer, by resemblance to the appointer, by success in a different role. Selection by bid asks candidates to state what they will deliver and to be exposed to the statement. It does not eliminate misjudgment — an overconfident bidder can win with a target they cannot reach — but it locates the consequence on the bidder rather than on the firm, and it resolves within the term of the contract rather than over a career.

The mechanism is paired with 官兵互选, mutual selection, which governs the same relationship from the other side: the unit's members participate in choosing among bidders, and may remove the holder subsequently. Grabbing the order is how the position is taken; mutual selection is how it is kept or lost.

Haier extended the same mechanism below the leadership position, so that members bid for work within a unit rather than being assigned it, and outside parties may bid for work the firm has posted. The general form is that any piece of work with a user attached to it can be opened rather than allocated.

The concept's boundaries: grabbing the order is not internal job posting, which invites applications for a manager to select among on ordinary criteria. It is not election, since the contest is decided by the commitment offered rather than by preference among candidates, and 官兵互选 is the separate election-like component. It is not self-selection or volunteering, since a bid must win against other bids and carries a contract the volunteer cannot decline once held.
