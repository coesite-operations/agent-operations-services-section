Binglian (并联) is the electrical term for a parallel circuit, used in Rendanheyi to name the arrangement of units within a chain group. Its opposite, 串联, is the series circuit, and the pair carries the model's structural argument by analogy.

Units in series are connected through each other. Each takes input from the unit before it, produces output for the unit after it, and reaches the user only through the whole remaining sequence. The arrangement has two properties the model objects to. Every junction is a place where a unit's obligation is defined by an internal party rather than by a user, so that discharging the obligation and serving the user are different acts. And the sequence is only as good as its worst link, while each link is individually assessed and can be individually blameless.

Units in parallel are each connected to the same two points — in the model's use, to the same user outcome and the same contract. The design unit, the manufacturing unit, and the market unit are not upstream and downstream of one another; they are simultaneously attached to one end user's value, and each is exposed to it directly. Their work still has a sequence in the ordinary practical sense; what is not sequenced is their accountability.

The analogy's substantive content is what happens at a junction. In series, the intermediate node is a payer, a specifier, and a point of local optimization. In parallel there is no intermediate node to be any of those things, because every unit terminates at the user. This is what the chain-group contract implements: one contract, all parties, one pool. Removing the internal junctions is the same operation as removing the internal payers, which is the same operation as satisfying zero distance for units that never touch a user.

The concept's boundaries: parallel wiring is not concurrency or parallel execution, which concern doing work at the same time rather than being accountable to the same thing — units in 并联 may well work in strict sequence. It is not a matrix structure, which adds a second reporting line and therefore a second internal party to satisfy. And it is not a cross-functional team, whose members remain attached in series to the departments that pay them.
