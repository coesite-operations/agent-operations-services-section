Duidu (对赌) is the contract by which a micro-enterprise commits to a target and accepts the consequences of the result in both directions. The term is borrowed from Chinese investment practice, where it names the valuation-adjustment clauses common in private financing; its literal sense is a mutual wager, and English-language accounts of Rendanheyi render it as a value adjustment mechanism, or VAM.

The contract states what the unit will achieve, over what period, measured how. Overshooting it pays the unit's members a share of the excess. Missing it costs them, materially — reduced or eliminated value-added pay, and for a sustained miss, the dissolution of the unit or the removal of its leader. Both directions are essential and the second is the one that distinguishes the instrument. A commitment with upside only is a bonus scheme, and a bonus scheme is a thing an organization can decline to pay without any structural consequence following.

The target is a 引领目标, a leading goal, and this is the second essential feature. It is set by reference to the best comparable performance available anywhere in the market, not by reference to the unit's own prior year. Incremental targets derived from history are the ordinary instrument of corporate planning, and the model rejects them on the ground that they measure a unit against its own past — which a declining business will meet all the way down, and which rewards a unit for having been bad before.

The instrument's function is to make the three rights survivable for the firm that grants them. A unit given decision, personnel, and distribution authority without exposure is a unit that can spend the firm's resources on its own judgment with no mechanism of correction short of revoking the rights. The wager is the correction: it is what allows authority to be delegated in full rather than provisionally, because the unit's results discipline it without anyone needing to intervene. The rights and the wager are therefore a single arrangement described from two sides.

Its known failure modes are worth recording, because the instrument is gameable in the ordinary ways. Units negotiate soft targets. Units decline work that would jeopardize a target already committed to. Units under a target they can no longer reach have no incentive to maximize within the shortfall. Haier's countermeasure to the first is that targets are benchmarked externally rather than negotiated internally; the remaining two are real and are not fully answered by the model's public descriptions.

The concept's boundaries: a value-adjustment contract is not a performance review, which is a judgment about a person rendered by another person. It is not a management-by-objectives target or an OKR, both of which are goal-setting practices without a symmetric financial consequence. It is not a budget, which allocates resources rather than committing to a result. It is a bet with the firm, entered voluntarily, that the unit can be made to lose.
