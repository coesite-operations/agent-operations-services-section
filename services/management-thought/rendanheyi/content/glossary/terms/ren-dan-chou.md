Rendanchou (人单酬) is the compensation rule of the Rendanheyi model: 酬, pay, is a function of 单, the user value created by 人, the person. Haier's own formulation is 薪酬来自用户 — salary comes from users, not from the company.

The rule is not a description of where the cash physically originates, which in any firm is customers. It is a statement about what determines the amount. Under ordinary compensation, pay is determined by position, tenure, market rate for the role, and a manager's assessment; the customer's money funds the payroll without any customer's decision changing what any individual receives. Under rendanchou the causal link is direct: the value a specific user received determines what the person who created it is paid, and no intervening judgment adjusts it.

Pay is structured in two components. Base pay (基本酬) is the floor. Value-added pay (超值酬) is a share of the surplus the unit created over its committed target, distributed by the unit under its own distribution right. The second component is the substantive one, and its defining property is that it goes to zero. A unit that created no user value pays no value-added compensation, irrespective of effort spent, hours worked, difficulty encountered, or the reasonableness of the excuse. The model is explicit that this must hold in the cases where it is painful, since a floor that is quietly raised when a good team has a bad year has restored the company as the payer and abolished the rule.

The rule's function is to remove the manager as the intermediary of income. Where a person's pay is set by a manager's judgment, the person's rational object of attention is that judgment, and the user is reached only insofar as the manager's judgment tracks the user — which is exactly the layer of inference the model regards as the source of large-firm dysfunction. Making pay a function of user value makes attention to the user the same act as attention to one's income, which is what 合一 in the model's name asserts.

Its implications for the shape of the firm are severe and are the reason the rule cannot be adopted alone. Pay set by user value cannot be reconciled with compensation bands, since a small unit serving users exceptionally will generate amounts no band contains. It cannot be reconciled with internal pay equity, since two people at the same level will earn very differently. And it cannot be reconciled with an approval requirement on compensation, since an approver is by definition an intervening judgment. This is why the rule is inseparable from the distribution right: a firm that keeps final authority over pay has kept the company as the payer whatever it says about users.

The concept's boundaries: rendanchou is not commission, which pays a fraction of revenue transacted and is available to sales roles only. It is not profit-sharing or gainsharing, which distribute a pool the company determines after the fact at a scale that leaves position as the dominant term in anyone's pay. It is not equity or an employee stock plan, which link pay to the firm's aggregate value rather than to a person's users. And it is not pay-for-performance, in which performance is a manager's assessment of a person — the whole content of the term is that the assessor is the user.
