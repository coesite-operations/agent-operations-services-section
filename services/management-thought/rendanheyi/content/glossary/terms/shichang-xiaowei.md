A shichang xiaowei (市场小微), or market micro-enterprise, is the kind of micro-enterprise that faces an end user directly. It owns a product line or a user base, and in English-language accounts of Rendanheyi it is also called a user micro-enterprise or a transforming micro-enterprise — the last because it typically transforms an existing business in place rather than starting a new one.

Its defining property is that its 单 is immediate. There is a person outside the firm who chooses whether to buy, and that person's decision arrives in the unit's accounts without passing through anyone's judgment about whether the unit performed well. This makes the market micro-enterprise the least theoretically difficult of the three kinds: the model's requirement of zero distance is satisfied by the unit's ordinary position rather than by any instrument.

Its constitution is the standard one. It holds the three rights, signs a value-adjustment contract against externally benchmarked targets, keeps a win-win value-added statement, and pays its members from user-created value. Haier's frequently cited instances include the Casarte premium appliance units and the Thunderobot gaming laptop business, the latter begun by a small group inside the firm and grown into an independent listed company.

The market micro-enterprise's characteristic obligation, and the one that distinguishes it from an ordinary product team with a P&L, is that it does not own its supply. It cannot direct the node micro-enterprises that serve it, because they are enterprises and not its subordinates, and it is free to buy the same capability outside the firm if they do not serve it well. What it owns is the user relationship; what it must obtain by contract is everything else. This is what places it in a chain group rather than at the top of a value chain.

The concept's boundaries: a market micro-enterprise is not a business unit or division, both of which face a market while remaining funded and directed by a parent. It is not a brand, which is a property rather than an organization. And its user-facing position confers no authority over the units it depends on — the model is explicit that facing the user makes a unit responsible to the user, not senior to its suppliers.
