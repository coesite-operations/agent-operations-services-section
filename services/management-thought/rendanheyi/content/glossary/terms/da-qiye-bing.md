Da qiye bing (大企业病), large-enterprise disease, is the syndrome of scale that Rendanheyi is constructed against. The phrase is common in Chinese management discourse and predates the model; Rendanheyi's contribution is a claim about its cause and therefore about its cure.

The syndrome's presentation is familiar. Decisions require assent from parties who will not bear their consequences. Units report performance against measures that improve while the business does not. Information moving upward is corrected at each step toward what the next step wants to hear. Effort concentrates on securing budget and headcount, which are the currency of internal standing. Nobody is individually derelict, and the firm as a whole is unable to act.

Rendanheyi's diagnosis is that this is not a cultural failure, a bureaucratic accretion, or a consequence of hiring the wrong people, and that it cannot be cured by exhortation, by reorganization, or by replacing the people. It is the correct behavior of rational people whose income, standing, and continued employment are determined internally. A person paid by the company, assessed by a manager, and funded by a budget is being asked to attend to the user as a matter of professional virtue while every mechanism that touches their life attends to the firm. The user loses this contest reliably, and the firm's size is what makes it a contest at all: below some scale, the same person can see the user directly, and no mechanism is needed for them to care.

The model's cure follows from the diagnosis. If the disease is caused by income being determined internally, then it is cured only by income being determined externally — hence pay from users, contracts to users, and accounts that measure users. Every other feature is a consequence: units small enough to have an attributable user, rights sufficient to act on that user's behalf, and no approving layer to attend to instead. The model's distinctive claim is that these are not remedies applied to a firm that has the disease, but the conditions under which a firm cannot contract it.

The disease's characteristic vector in an otherwise-reformed firm is the captive internal customer. A unit whose customer cannot leave has an internal payer whatever the firm's stated model, and it will exhibit the full syndrome inside a company that has abolished the syndrome everywhere else. This is why the node micro-enterprise carries the model's heaviest requirements — the chain-group contract that passes its 单 through to an end user, and bilateral market exposure so that its internal customers are free to buy elsewhere and it is free to sell elsewhere. Both exist to deny the node the captive payer that is the disease's remaining foothold.

The concept's boundaries: large-enterprise disease is not bureaucracy in the Weberian sense, which is a form of administration that can be effective at what it is for. It is not organizational inertia or resistance to change, which are descriptions of a firm's response to a proposal rather than of its ordinary operation. It is not complacency or a failure of urgency, which locate the cause in the disposition of the people — the term's whole diagnostic content is that the people are behaving sensibly given what they are paid by.
