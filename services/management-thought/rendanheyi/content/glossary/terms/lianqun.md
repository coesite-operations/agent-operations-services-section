A lianqun (链群) is a self-organizing group of micro-enterprises that together span everything needed to serve one end user. It is rendered in English as a chain group, and in Haier's own English materials as an ecosystem micro-community, or EMC. The term entered the model's vocabulary in the 2019 restructuring and substantially displaced the earlier platform-centered grouping.

The word is a compound of 链, chain, and 群, group or swarm, and the compound is the point. A chain implies sequence and therefore handoffs, each with its own contract, its own margin, and its own opportunity to optimize locally at the next link's expense. A group implies simultaneity without direction. A chain group is meant to be a set of units that covers a chain's full span while contracting as a group.

Its defining instrument is that the group holds one contract to the end user rather than a series of contracts to each other. Every constituent unit — including node micro-enterprises that never touch that user — is a party to that single contract and is paid out of the same value-added when the group overshoots its target. This is the arrangement Haier describes as 并联, wiring in parallel: the units are pegged to a common outcome rather than connected in series, so that no unit can succeed on an internal measure while the group fails on the user's.

The consequence is a change in what a supplying unit is optimizing. Under serial internal contracts, a design unit's objective is the satisfaction of the manufacturing unit it hands to, which is an internal political signal. Under a chain-group contract, the design unit's objective is the end user's value, which it happens to reach through manufacturing. The internal unit remains who it serves and stops being who it answers to.

Haier's descriptions distinguish two components within a chain group: an experience chain group (体验链群), which holds the user relationship and is responsible for the value experienced, and a solution or creation chain group (创单链群), which builds what that requires. These are the group's two faces rather than two organizations, and they share the contract.

Chain groups are self-organizing in the specific sense that no authority composes them. Units join a chain group by contracting into it and leave by contracting out, and a group that cannot attract the units it needs does not get formed. There is no headquarters function that assigns membership, and no appeal to one when a needed unit declines.

The concept's boundaries. A chain group is not a value chain, which is an analytical description of a sequence of activities rather than a contracting party. It is not a business unit or a division, both of which are composed by a parent and can be recomposed by it. It is not a cross-functional team, which draws its members from departments that continue to employ and pay them. And it is not a joint venture or consortium, since its members are not contracting *with each other* — the group's whole structural claim is that they are contracting *together, with a user*.

The term's history is a genuine source of confusion in the literature. Haier's organizational vocabulary shifted materially across the 2013, 2016, and 2019 restructurings, and 链群 was introduced without a clean public statement of what became of the platform category it largely replaced. Accounts written on either side of 2019 therefore use platform and chain group in ways that do not reconcile, and neither is straightforwardly wrong about the period it describes.
