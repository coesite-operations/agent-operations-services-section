Ling qianzi (零签字), zero signature, is the rule that a micro-enterprise's actions require no approval from above. It is the operational face of the three rights: a unit that genuinely holds decision, personnel, and distribution authority has nobody to ask, and the absence of the signature is the observable evidence that the rights were transferred rather than lent.

The rule's strength is in what it asserts, which is not that approval is fast or that authority is broadly delegated but that no approving party exists. This distinction is the one firms erase in adopting the vocabulary. Raising approval thresholds, granting spending limits, and empowering managers to decide within bands all leave an approver in place and merely reduce how often they are consulted; the approver's judgment remains the thing the unit is ultimately accountable to, and it reasserts itself at exactly the moments that matter — the unusual decision, the large commitment, the departure from precedent. Zero signature says that in the unit's ordinary domain there is nothing to reassert, because the authority is not held anywhere above the unit.

What replaces the signature is the contract. A unit that acts without approval is not thereby unaccountable; it has committed to a target under a value-adjustment contract, keeps accounts showing what it created, and bears the consequence of the result. Approval and exposure are alternative mechanisms of control, and the model's position is that they are substitutes rather than complements: approval controls the decision in advance by a party who knows less than the decider, exposure controls it afterward by the result. A firm that wants both has not removed the approver, and the unit will optimize for the approver, since the approver acts first.

The rule's cost is that mistakes are made and are not caught by anyone above. The model accepts this explicitly, on the ground that the approval layer's catch rate on genuine errors is low, its cost in latency and in unattempted actions is high, and its real function is usually to distribute blame rather than to prevent loss.

The concept's boundaries: zero signature is not delegated authority or a raised approval limit, both of which retain an approver. It is not a flat structure, which describes the number of layers rather than where the authority sits. It is not autonomy or empowerment, which are latitudes granted and therefore withdrawable. And it is not a claim that the unit answers to nobody — it answers to its contract and to its users, which is a harder counterparty than a signature.
