Jingshen lingxiu (精神领袖), "spiritual leader," names an organizational office whose authority is cultural and ideological rather than executive.
The holder sets and guards the organization's direction, values, and self-understanding, and does not run its daily business.

The office is defined by a deliberate separation.
In an ordinary corporation the founder's personal authority and the chief executive's operational authority are fused in one person, so the founder's judgment becomes the company's daily decision procedure.
The spiritual leader splits these apart: operational command is delegated to an executive body that decides by its own process, while the founder retains the authority to say what the organization is for.

Huawei is the working example.
Ren Zhengfei founded the company in 1987 and holds only a small fraction of its shares — under one and a half percent, with the remainder held by an employee shareholding scheme.
He does not direct daily operations, which belong to the rotating chairmanship and the executive management team.
What he retains is the power to define and defend the company's founding direction, expressed through internal letters, speeches, and articles that are circulated as the company's authoritative self-description, and a veto over the governing bodies that he has described as reserved for the rare case where the company's fundamental direction is at stake rather than used as a routine instrument.
His stated intent is that the veto lapses and the office becomes purely one of direction-setting.

The mechanism is that authority over meaning and authority over operations decay differently.
Operational authority must be exercised constantly and therefore must rotate, be delegated, and be checked, or it concentrates and calcifies.
Authority over meaning is exercised rarely and loses force if spent on ordinary decisions.
Separating the two lets the organization be run by an institution while still being oriented by a person.

The office's boundaries matter.
A spiritual leader is not a figurehead: the authority is real, and it binds the executive body on questions of direction.
It is not a chairman of the board, which is an executive and legal office with fiduciary duties.
It is not a chief executive, and specifically not one who has merely retreated to an advisory posture while still making operational calls informally — that arrangement is the fusion the office exists to prevent.
It is also not hereditary or transferable by appointment; the authority derives from having founded the thing and from continuous demonstrated fidelity to its purpose, which is why the office typically ends rather than passes.
