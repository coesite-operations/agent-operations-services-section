Dao (道), "the Way," names the highest level of an organization's reasoning: its fundamental direction and governing purpose, the thing all its methods, systems, and tactics exist to serve.

In management use, dao answers what the organization is for and which way it faces.
It is deliberately few in number and slow to change.
Huawei's dao is stated as customer-centricity — the claim that the company exists to create value for customers, and that everything else, including its own survival, follows from that rather than competing with it.
A statement at this level is not a slogan or an aspiration but a decision procedure: when a concrete choice arises, the dao is what the choice is checked against, and a method that conflicts with it is discarded no matter how well it performs on its own terms.

Dao is the highest of a set of four levels that a management decision can sit at.
Below it is the level of institution and codified rule; below that the level of technique and method; and alongside these the level of positional advantage and momentum.
The ordering is strict and is the point: technique serves institution, institution serves direction, and none of the lower levels may override the highest.
An organization that inverts this — letting a technique that works, or a favorable position, redefine what it is for — has lost its dao, and the classical formulation holds that it will then optimize itself into incoherence, since it no longer has a standard against which any optimization can be judged wrong.

A recurring maxim in this frame is that the great Way is utterly simple.
The claim is that a genuine dao can be stated plainly and briefly, and that elaboration is a symptom: a direction requiring a long document to express is usually several competing directions that have not yet been reconciled, and it cannot function as a standard because it cannot be applied without interpretation.

The concept's boundaries matter.
Dao is not strategy — a strategy is a plan for a period, chosen and revised against the dao, whereas the dao is what the revising is done in service of.
It is not values or culture in the sense of stated dispositions; it is directional, naming what the organization moves toward.
It is not a mission statement written for outside readers, since its function is internal and adjudicative.
And it is not mystical: in this use the word carries no metaphysical claim, and names simply the level at which purpose is settled and from which everything below takes its warrant.
