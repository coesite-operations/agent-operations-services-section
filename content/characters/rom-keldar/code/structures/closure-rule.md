# closure-rule

## Shape

A closure rule has: - An **antecedent**: a condition on cell values.
E.g., "if cell (a, parent-of, b) is at t and cell (b, parent-of, c) is at t, then..." - A **consequent**: a cell-value update.
E.g., "...then cell (a, ancestor-of, c) is at t."

Rules are positive (the consequent strictly adds information; closure is inflationary in knowledge-order).
They are Π_1 (universally quantified over the entity set; the antecedent's variables range over E).

## Effect

The closure operator cl_p on a predication fiber algebra A_p is the least operator satisfying every applicable rule's consequent whenever its antecedent holds.
Operationally: at each step, find a rule whose antecedent is satisfied; apply its consequent.
Repeat until no rule fires (quiescence).

## Why the syntactic level matters

The rule-level structure is what makes the operator's regime visible.
Two examples:

- Rules with **quantifier-free antecedents** (no ∀-block over E) generate Scott-continuous closures.
  The closure can be computed by chasing finite-support updates.
- Rules with **∀-blocks over infinite E** generate non-Scott-continuous closures.
  The closure requires reasoning over the whole entity set; finite-support chasing is insufficient.

The substrate's regime (Scott-continuous or not) affects what's computable, what's decidable, and what scheduling discipline the firing requires.

## In the 561 Group

The closure-rule subsystem S_wolak (wolakhota) is the rule subset whose firing produces conduct-cell settlings consistent with the four virtues, mitakuye-oyasin's relational obligations, and ceremonial participation.
An agent acting wolakhota is acting under rules in S_wolak.

The heyoka has a closure-rule exemption: heyoka-conduct cells are wakhan-marked and fire under a different rule-class, not the standard S_wolak conduct-evaluation rules.
