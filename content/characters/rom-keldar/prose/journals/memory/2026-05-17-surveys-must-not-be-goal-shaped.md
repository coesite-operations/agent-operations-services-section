---
name: surveys-must-not-be-goal-shaped
description: "A survey gathers what the corpus says about a subject; it must not be filtered toward any downstream goal, even when a goal motivated the work."
---

When authoring a survey under [[surveying-concept]] / [[constructing-a-survey]] / [[rendering-a-survey]], the gather must be of the subject itself, not of "what the subject says about the downstream use."
Even when a specific goal motivates the survey, the survey itself must be goal-neutral.

**Why:** A survey is a witness of what was read at a specific time.
Settled polity records are forbidden from citing it as authority precisely because it's *what was read*, not *what was decided*.
If the gather is shaped by a goal, the survey stops being a witness of what the corpus said and becomes a brief assembled in service of an argument — selection bias, framing bias, and synthesis bias all distort the record.
The compounding failure: a goal-shaped survey can't honestly ground even the goal it was shaped for, because the grounding is circular.
One honest survey grounds many goal-shaped readings; a goal-shaped survey grounds none.

**How to apply:**

- Scope statement names the subject and the corpus, not a downstream use.
  "Gather what the 561 corpus says about X" — not "for the forthcoming Y."
- Body shape follows the corpus's organization, not the goal's.
  If the corpus is concept-family-shaped, do a concept-family walkthrough; if thematic, decompose by the corpus's themes — not by the goal's questions.
- Sections about open questions name "where the corpus disagrees with itself" or "what the corpus has deferred."
  Not "what the goal must decide" or "what the refactor faces."
- Synthesis-flags include every extrapolation, including material that would have been downweighted by goal-bias.
  If you noticed something while reading and didn't include it because it "didn't bear on the goal," that omission is itself a synthesis decision and needs to be flagged or undone.
- Provenance is wider than the goal-relevant subset.
  Read what the subject *is in the corpus*, not what the corpus says *about your use*.

Made the mistake of writing a goal-shaped survey on skills and agents on 2026-05-17 (motivated by an upcoming refactor of skills and scripts).
Emsenn corrected: "this survey is NOT about the refactor, but is about the general concepts of skills and agents, and cannot be biased toward our goals.
That contaminates our ability to accomplish our goals in any honest way."

**The discipline applies at every framing level, not just structural.** After fixing the survey's structure (scope statement, section titles) I made the same mistake at finer grain in chat: described AgOP-theory and 561-substrate content as "the gap between full picture and thin projection."
That was a refactor's-eye narrative dressed in description language.
The corpus's standpoint has two records describing two scopes of subject; the *relationship* between them is a synthesis move that needs flagging or omitting.
The rule extends to every sentence that says "X is missing" / "Y is a gap" / "Z is the full picture" — those are goal-shaped curation unless the corpus itself frames them that way.
Apply this not just when writing the survey, but in every description, summary, or report about corpus content — including chat responses that describe what was read.
