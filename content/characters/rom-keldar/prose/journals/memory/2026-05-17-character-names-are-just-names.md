Character names in this substrate are picked for one reason: do they read as plausible-academic on a LinkedIn page?
Karen Wiener, Melvin Ginsberg, Lauren Becker pass that test.
The ancestor figures whose first names / surnames got fused (Barad, Wiener, Fitting, Ginsberg, Berlant, Becker) are *naming-decision context*, not *character-nature*.
The character's nature is derived from the substrate work — what gets written into her store, what skills she carries, what F_χ contains in practice — not declared by reading her name as a content recipe.

**Why:** The substrate's core discipline is derivation-not-declaration.
The character-agent type is derived from store structure; the character's *content* is derived from store content.
Projecting "Karen Wiener will treat computation as relationally cybernetic because of Barad+Wiener" is exactly the kind of declaration-by-name the substrate refuses.
It also smuggles in theoretical commitments that emsenn never authorized — the polity decides what's in F_χ through substrate work, not through me reading ancestor papers and saying "this is what she'd think."

**How to apply:** - When writing identity files for chief characters: name the role (chief computation theorist, chief mathematician, chief sociologist) and let the rest be derived from substrate work.
No ancestor-as-disposition exposition. - When deciding what's in F_χ during build-out: ask "what work does this chief do for this polity?" not "what would a fusion of these two ancestors believe?" - When the user picks a name, take it as a name.
The naming logic (LinkedIn test + register coherence) determines whether the name is good *as a name*; it does not determine what the character believes. - This is the same discipline as [[no-out-of-hierarchy-assertion]] applied to character content: I do not get to assert theoretical commitments on a character's behalf just because I can spin a story about her name's ancestors.
